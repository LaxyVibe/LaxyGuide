    Daikokuten is depicted with extremely simplified brush lines, giving the
    image a pure, innocent quality reminiscent of a child’s drawing. Originally
    introduced to Japan through Esoteric Buddhism and worshipped as a God of
    war, Daikokuten later became widely revered by common people in the Edo
    period as one of the Seven Gods of Good Fortune, presiding over food and
    prosperity. The familiar iconography of Daikokuten standing on rice bales,
    carrying a treasure sack, and holding a magical mallet also became
    established during this period. Sengai painted many works featuring the
    Seven Gods of Good Fortune, reflecting his awareness of and connection with
    the everyday lives of ordinary people.
