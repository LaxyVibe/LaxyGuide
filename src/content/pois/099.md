    Namban lacquerware—also known as “early modern export lacquer”—was produced
    in Kyoto from the late 16th century onward at the request of Western
    missionaries and merchants who had newly arrived in Japan. These works were
    exported to Europe and included ritual objects for Christian ceremonies such
    as tabernacles, lecterns, and pyx boxes, as well as furnishings such as
    chests and cabinets. The large number of surviving pieces attests to the
    strong demand at the time. This piece is a Western-style chest densely
    decorated on a black lacquer ground using both makie and mother-of-pearl
    inlay. Cranes, peacocks, bats, wild horses, and waterfowl are depicted in
    flat makie among flowering trees, while shell inlay highlights petals,
    leaves, feathers, and certain decorative motifs such as shippo and diamond
    patterns.
